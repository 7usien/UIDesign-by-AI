# Conversion and Proof

Design the page around the visitor's decision, not a fixed section template.

## Build the decision path

Answer in the order the audience needs:

1. Is this for me?
2. What meaningful outcome is offered?
3. Why should I believe it?
4. How does it work or what will I receive?
5. Why choose this approach?
6. What risk or objection remains?
7. What should I do next?

Reorder, combine, or omit sections when the buying context warrants it.

If traffic source or campaign context is known, preserve message scent: the hero promise, terminology, and offer must continue what brought the visitor to the page.

## Use the strongest available proof

Prefer, in order:

- observable product behavior or real work;
- approved outcome data and case evidence;
- customer evidence with permission;
- concrete deliverables, methods, or expertise;
- transparent limitations and process.

Treat unverified numbers, logos, quotes, awards, certifications, and integrations as unavailable. Do not style placeholders as proof.

## Make action coherent

- Use one primary conversion action and a genuinely different secondary action only when needed.
- Match CTA commitment to visitor readiness.
- Make the CTA label describe the next step.
- Repeat the action after meaningful evidence, not mechanically after every section.
- Ensure the final CTA resolves the narrative rather than acting as a decorative banner.
- Put objection handling and risk reduction near the decision they affect. Do not invent guarantees, free trials, cancellation terms, or refund policies.

Clarity is not generic copy. Prefer concrete language about the audience, change, artifact, or next step over inflated slogans.
