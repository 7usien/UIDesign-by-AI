# Design Principles

Use these principles to make context-specific decisions. They are evaluation criteria, not a reusable visual style.

## Content and conversion

- Make the hero explain what the offer is, who it serves, the primary outcome, and the next action within seconds.
- Organize the page around the visitor’s decision: promise, credible proof, benefits, objections, trust, and action.
- Prefer real product UI, diagrams, demonstrations, cases, and approved data over decorative dashboards.
- Omit unavailable proof instead of fabricating customers, testimonials, statistics, awards, integrations, or compliance.

## Art direction

- Connect the visual premise to the product, audience, positioning, and supplied references.
- Create one dominant idea per viewport; let hierarchy, type, composition, and whitespace carry more weight than effects.
- Use a restrained token system with intentional color, spacing, radius, and elevation scales.
- Use no more than two font families unless brand or language requirements justify another.
- Avoid designs that could belong to any company after changing only the logo and text.

## Interaction and motion

- Use interaction to demonstrate product behavior, reveal useful detail, or improve feedback.
- Avoid continuous or cursor-driven motion when it does not strengthen comprehension.
- Preserve keyboard access, visible focus, touch comfort, reduced motion, and stable layout.

## Responsive and RTL

- Design mobile hierarchy and section order deliberately instead of merely stacking desktop blocks.
- Keep primary proof and the main CTA discoverable without excessive mobile scrolling.
- For Arabic, verify typography, line wrapping, direction, icon orientation, numerals, and mixed Arabic/Latin content independently from English.

## Anti-patterns

- Generic dark SaaS themes, random gradients, and repeated glass cards.
- Bento grids used without information-architecture need.
- Fake dashboards, activity feeds, counters, testimonials, or logo marquees.
- Excessive badges, nested cards, tiny metadata, and decorative code panels.
- Animation libraries added before a concrete motion requirement exists.
