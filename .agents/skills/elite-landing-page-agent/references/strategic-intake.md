# Strategic Intake

## Inspect before asking

Extract what is already known from the request, repository, current page, assets, and supplied references:

- offer and category;
- primary audience and buying context;
- problem, desired outcome, and differentiator;
- primary conversion action;
- approved proof and assets;
- language, direction, voice, and technical constraints.

Do not ask the user to repeat discoverable information.

## Ask only material questions

If critical information remains missing, ask one compact batch with no more than five questions:

1. What exactly is offered, and who is the highest-priority visitor?
2. What should that visitor do next?
3. Which claims, results, customers, screenshots, or other proof may be shown?
4. Which reference URLs or images should influence the work, and what feels relevant about them?
5. Which languages, brand constraints, or non-negotiable exclusions apply?

Omit answered questions. Accept rough answers; do not turn intake into a workshop.

## Freeze a working brief

Before art direction, record a compact brief containing:

- audience situation;
- specific promise;
- primary and secondary CTA;
- proof inventory with `approved`, `observed`, `placeholder`, or `missing` status;
- objections to resolve;
- references and exclusions;
- language/direction matrix;
- implementation constraints.

Never convert an inference into an approved claim. Missing proof changes the page strategy: show honest deliverables, process, product behavior, or founder expertise instead of invented validation.
